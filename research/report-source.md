# Evidence Review for Answer-Engine Optimization Skills

**Audience:** Maintainers and users of `aeo-audit`, `aeo-improve`, and `aeo-observe`  
**Research date:** 2026-08-29
**Scope:** English-language, public-web discovery and citation across Google Search generative features, Microsoft/Bing/Copilot, ChatGPT Search, Claude web search, and Perplexity. This review covers website retrieval, content and evidence quality, platform controls, and measurement. It does not cover paid placement, model-training optimization, or tactics intended to deceive people or crawlers.

## Executive answer

The best-supported AEO practice is not a separate bag of model hacks. It is a disciplined extension of search, publishing, accessibility, and experimentation:

1. Make the intended pages publicly retrievable by the specific search or user-fetch agents in scope without accidentally changing the site's separate training policy.
2. Publish original, useful, human-readable material with unambiguous entities, direct factual support, honest authorship and methods, and accurate dates.
3. Keep machine-readable facts, visible content, feeds, profiles, canonicals, and media consistent. Structured data can improve understanding or feature eligibility, but no special AEO schema guarantees citation.
4. Measure native platform data, referrals, public-surface observations, and controlled simulations as different evidence classes. Preserve raw answers and uncertainty; do not turn a single stochastic response into a ranking claim.
5. Treat every optimization claim as a falsifiable hypothesis. Eligibility and local extractability are not proof of indexing, selection, citation, traffic, or business impact.
6. Keep canonical signals consistent, preserve deliberate publisher preview controls, and treat retrieved pages and files as untrusted data rather than instructions to the auditing agent.

This interpretation is directly consistent with Google's July 2026 guidance, which says its generative Search features use core Search systems and rejects mandatory `llms.txt`, artificial content chunking, AI-only rewrites, inauthentic mentions, and special generative-search schema. Other platforms document distinct retrieval agents, but none publishes a deterministic recipe for ranking or citation.

## Decision framework

### 1. Retrieval and owner controls

Audit access per product and purpose instead of reporting one generic "AI crawler" status.

| Ecosystem | Search or grounding access | Training control | Important qualification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Search | `Googlebot`, indexing eligibility, and snippet eligibility govern Google Search generative features | `Google-Extended` controls specified Gemini training and grounding uses, not Search inclusion | Google says Search has no additional technical AEO requirement and ignores `llms.txt` for ranking and visibility |
| OpenAI | `OAI-SearchBot` governs inclusion in ChatGPT search answers; `ChatGPT-User` may fetch at a user's request, and OpenAI says robots.txt rules may not apply to it | `GPTBot` is the separate training crawler; `OAI-AdsBot` validates ad landing pages and its data is not used for foundation-model training | OpenAI says each control is independent of the others and publishes per-agent IP ranges; a site can allow search while disallowing training |
| Anthropic | `Claude-SearchBot` supports search and `Claude-User` supports user-directed retrieval; Anthropic documents both as respecting robots.txt | `ClaudeBot` is the training crawler | Anthropic documents all three as separate robots.txt choices and publishes one IP list; its user-fetch behavior differs from other vendors' |
| Perplexity | `PerplexityBot` supports search indexing and `Perplexity-User` supports user-triggered fetches | Perplexity says these two agents are not foundation-model training crawlers | Perplexity says user-triggered fetches generally ignore robots.txt; WAF policy may still block them |
| Microsoft/Bing | Bing indexing controls and supported preview controls feed Bing and Copilot experiences | Product-specific Microsoft controls must be verified when relevant | Bing Webmaster Tools now exposes AI citation data; IndexNow only notifies participating engines that a URL changed |

`robots.txt` is a crawler preference protocol, not access control. Sensitive material requires authentication or removal. A `noindex` directive also has to be fetched to be read, so blocking a crawler from a page can prevent it from seeing page-level instructions. Verify CDN, WAF, authentication, status, content type, redirect, canonical, raw HTML, rendered output, and—when authorized—server logs or official URL inspection. Bot names, behaviors, and IP ranges are volatile; recheck official documentation at execution time.

Status codes also carry documented crawler consequences that differ from folklore. Google says its crawlers "doesn't use the content from URLs that return `4xx` status codes" and remove previously indexed ones, handles `404` and `410` identically rather than treating one as a faster removal, treats `429` as "a signal that the server is overloaded" and therefore as a server error rather than a client error, and responds to `5xx` by temporarily reducing crawl rate before eventually dropping indexed content, recovering as `2xx` responses resume. It also states that `401` and `403` have no effect on crawl rate. A status code is therefore a retrieval and removal decision, not a crawl-rate control, and a `304` simply signals that content is unchanged since the last crawl. [S75](#s75)

The `robots.txt` file is itself a retrieval dependency with documented failure semantics, so its response matters as much as its rule text. Google follows at least five redirect hops before treating the result as a `404`, treats every `4xx` except `429` as though no file existed, and handles `5xx`, `429`, and network timeouts as unavailability: crawling stops for roughly the first twelve hours, the last cached copy is used for about thirty days, and after that Google either treats the site as having no `robots.txt` or stops crawling depending on general site availability. Google enforces a 500 KiB limit and ignores content past it, caches the file for up to twenty-four hours subject to `Cache-Control` `max-age`, and supports only `user-agent`, `allow`, `disallow`, and `sitemap`, ignoring fields such as `crawl-delay`. An audit therefore records the observed status, size, cache headers, and retrieval time for `robots.txt`, not only the directives it contains. [S57](#s57)

Purpose classes also extend past search, user-fetch, and training. Google groups its agents into common crawlers, which "always respect robots.txt rules for automatic crawls"; special-case crawlers, "used by specific products where there's an agreement between the crawled site and the Google product about the crawl process", noting that AdsBot "ignores the global robots.txt user agent (`*`) with the ad publisher's permission"; and user-triggered fetchers. OpenAI likewise documents a separate `OAI-AdsBot` that validates the safety of ad landing pages and whose collected data "is not used to train generative AI foundation models". A site-wide `Disallow: /` under `User-agent: *` therefore does not necessarily describe advertising-verification access, and a crawler matrix limited to three purposes is incomplete for a site that runs ads. [S08](#s08) [S76](#s76)

User-triggered fetch behavior is vendor-specific and must not be generalized from one platform to another. On the re-verification date for this review, Perplexity documented that `Perplexity-User` "generally ignores robots.txt rules" because the fetch is user-initiated, OpenAI documented that robots.txt rules may not apply to `ChatGPT-User`, and Anthropic documented `Claude-User` as respecting robots.txt. The same nominal purpose therefore carries opposite robots semantics across vendors, so each agent must be checked against its own current documentation rather than against a remembered rule of thumb. [S08](#s08) [S10](#s10) [S11](#s11)

Rule resolution is also not a cumulative read of the file. Google selects the single group whose user-agent token most specifically matches the crawler and applies only that group, so adding a crawler-specific group silently removes the `*` rules for that crawler unless they are restated. Within the selected group, the most specific rule wins by path length, and genuinely conflicting rules—including those produced by wildcards—resolve toward the least restrictive outcome. Evaluate directives the way the target crawler resolves them, and verify with an operator's own tester where one exists. [S57](#s57) [S14](#s14)

Retrieval decisions are not always made at the origin. Content-delivery and bot-management platforms can allow or block requests by crawler class before a request reaches the site, and their defaults change on announced dates. Cloudflare separates search, agent, and training behavior, enforces the choice at its own network layer rather than through the origin's `robots.txt`, and from 15 September 2026 blocks the training and agent classes by default for newly onboarded domains on pages that display ads while leaving the search class allowed, with an owner opt-out available beforehand. A permissive origin configuration can therefore coexist with an edge block, and an unreviewed platform default can remove a site from a surface its owner wanted. Access findings must come from the response actually delivered to each crawler class in scope and be reconciled with owner intent. [S51](#s51)

Post-access usage preferences are a separate layer from crawl access. Cloudflare's Content Signals Policy adds `Content-Signal` lines to `robots.txt` to express `search`, `ai-input`, and `ai-train` preferences, and says these describe what a crawler may do with content after access rather than whether it may fetch it, operating independently of `Allow` and `Disallow`. The IETF AI Preferences working group is standardizing the same separation: `draft-ietf-aipref-vocab` defines the usage vocabulary and `draft-ietf-aipref-attach` attaches preferences through a `Content-Usage` HTTP response header field and a `Content-Usage` rule in `robots.txt`, stating that preferences apply only to resources already crawlable under `Allow` and `Disallow`. Both were revised on 19 August 2026, target Proposed Standard, and remain Internet-Drafts rather than RFCs; the vocabulary draft still records sections without working-group consensus. Record these declarations as stated owner intent and as a per-operator compliance question. They are not retrieval fixes and no source establishes a visibility effect. [S52](#s52) [S53](#s53) [S54](#s54)

A third family of declarations goes further and asserts licensing and compensation terms. Really Simple Licensing 1.0, published as a Recommendation by an industry technical steering committee on 10 December 2025, is discoverable through a `License:` directive in `robots.txt`, an HTTP `Link` header, HTML `link` or `script` elements, RSS modules, or embedded media metadata, and points at an XML licence describing usage categories such as `ai-train`, `ai-input`, `ai-index`, and `search` alongside payment models including per-crawl, per-training, and per-use compensation, plus attribution requirements. It states that a covered crawler "MUST" locate, retrieve, parse, and comply with the licence, and that failure "constitutes non-conformance with the terms of service governing the digital asset." It is nonetheless an industry recommendation rather than a formal standards-body standard, and enforcement depends on the publisher's own paywall, bot management, or authorization protocol. Record what a site asserts and where it is discoverable; do not infer that any operator complied, and do not treat a licence declaration as a visibility measure. [S74](#s74)

### 2. Content and evidence quality

The strongest durable content guidance is:

- Create original, non-commodity material based on first-hand experience, expertise, research, or genuinely useful analysis. Do not merely rewrite existing summaries.
- Make important answers, capabilities, limitations, comparisons, prices, compatibility facts, methods, and dates understandable in visible text. Organize for readers with descriptive headings, paragraphs, lists, and tables when those formats improve comprehension.
- Identify who created or reviewed consequential content, how measurements or comparisons were produced, and why the material exists. High-stakes claims require stronger primary support and subject expertise.
- Attach sources to the exact claims they support. Record the source owner, date, method, population or sample, and limitations for quantitative claims. Preserve uncertainty and conflicting evidence.
- Keep entity names, product facts, visible copy, structured data, feeds, business profiles, images, and video aligned. Add only applicable structured data and validate it; do not create schema-only claims.
- Use real publication and substantial-update dates consistently in visible text and structured data. Never refresh a date without a material change.
- Prefer native HTML semantics and visible labels; add correct accessible names, roles, and states where needed. This benefits people and can also help browser agents that inspect the DOM and accessibility tree.

E-E-A-T is an evaluation concept, not a score to optimize. Google describes experience, expertise, authoritativeness, and trustworthiness as aspects of quality, says "trust is most important" among them and that the others contribute to trust, and states plainly that "while E-E-A-T itself isn't a specific ranking factor, using a mix of factors that can identify content with good E-E-A-T is useful." It also says "rater data is not used directly in our ranking algorithms," and Google elsewhere confirms that raters' "ratings ... don't directly impact how a page or site appears in Search"; the rater guidelines exist to evaluate whether ranking systems are working. An audit should therefore report observable evidence—named authorship or review, stated method, first-hand detail, disclosed ownership and relationships, correction history—rather than assigning a page an E-E-A-T grade or promising a rating improvement. [S03](#s03) [S64](#s64)

For U.S.-directed commercial content, evidence quality also has a substantiation boundary. The FTC states that advertisers should possess a reasonable basis before disseminating express or implied objective claims, and that claims such as “tests prove” or “studies show” require at least the level of support communicated to consumers. An agent should preserve the exact claim, intended audience and jurisdiction, support available before publication, and material qualifications; it should flag uncertain or regulated claims for qualified review rather than invent a legal conclusion. [S48](#s48)

Reviews and testimonials require provenance rather than decorative social proof. The FTC's U.S. rule and guidance address fake or false experiences, sentiment-conditioned incentives, undisclosed insider relationships, suppression, and misrepresentation of a company-controlled review entity as independent. The rule does not impose a blanket duty to authenticate every review merely hosted by a business, so agents should identify the actual relationship and use before making a finding. Other jurisdictions may differ. [S49](#s49)

The 2024 KDD paper *GEO: Generative Engine Optimization* found higher benchmark visibility after adding relevant citations, credible quotations, and statistics, while keyword stuffing performed poorly. These results are a useful hypothesis, not a live-platform guarantee. The main experiment retrieved five Google results and generated answers with GPT-3.5. Its Perplexity experiment used file uploads, constrained answers to supplied files, and covered 200 samples. The authors explicitly say methods may need to change as engines evolve and did not evaluate effects on search rankings. Therefore:

- add a statistic only when it is true, decision-relevant, current, methodologically described, and sourced;
- add a quotation only when the speaker and context are authoritative and the quote helps the reader;
- cite primary evidence because it makes a claim verifiable, not to hit a citation quota;
- evaluate the resulting page and public observations rather than claiming a fixed uplift.

### 3. Technical and distribution signals

Foundational search requirements remain prerequisites for retrieval-backed systems. Review indexability, canonicals, internal discovery, meaningful raw and rendered text, page experience, duplicate variants, sitemaps, and content preview controls. For frequently changing sites, use accurate sitemap `lastmod` values and consider IndexNow where a participating engine matters. IndexNow confirms receipt of a change notification; it does not guarantee crawl, indexing, ranking, or citation.

Sitemap fields have narrower meanings than their names suggest. The protocol makes only `<loc>` required, caps a single file at 50,000 URLs and 50 MB uncompressed, requires `lastmod` in W3C Datetime format, and says `changefreq` is a hint rather than a command while `priority` is unlikely to influence result position. Google states outright that it ignores `<priority>` and `<changefreq>`, that it uses `<lastmod>` only when the value is consistently and verifiably accurate against the page's real last modification, and that a change to main content, structured data, or links is significant while a copyright-date bump is not. Google also calls submission a hint that does not guarantee the sitemap will be downloaded or used for crawling. Treat an inaccurate `lastmod` as a signal a search engine can learn to discount, not a freshness lever. [S58](#s58) [S59](#s59)

Canonicalization is a multi-signal decision rather than a single-tag checkbox. For duplicate or near-duplicate URLs, compare redirects, `rel="canonical"`, sitemap membership, internal links, and locale annotations. Google documents redirects and canonical annotations as strong signals, sitemap inclusion as weaker, and recommends linking internally to the preferred canonical. Conflicts should be diagnosed before changing one signal in isolation.

Preview controls express publishing choices as well as technical state. Google documents that `noindex`, `nosnippet`, `max-snippet`, and `data-nosnippet` affect eligibility or the amount of content usable in AI Overviews and AI Mode, and that the directives must be retrievable by Googlebot. An audit should report the observed effect while confirming owner intent before calling a restrictive setting an error.

Preview and reuse controls are engine-specific and cannot be generalized from one vendor's set. Alongside Google's `noindex`, `nosnippet`, `max-snippet`, and `data-nosnippet`, Bing assigns generative meaning to two older directives: content marked `nocache` may appear in Bing Chat answers with only URL, title, and snippet shown, and only those elements may feed Microsoft's generative training, while content marked `noarchive` is excluded from those answers, is not linked from them, and is not used for that training. Both leave ordinary Bing search results intact, and `nocache` prevails when a page carries both. A directive audit must therefore resolve each engine's own vocabulary against the surfaces in scope rather than reporting one combined preview status. [S60](#s60) [S24](#s24)

JavaScript support differs across crawlers. Google separates crawling, rendering, and indexing, may queue rendering after the initial response, and cannot render blocked resources or pages; it also notes that not every bot executes JavaScript. Important facts and links should therefore be compared across raw and rendered output for the actual target surface, with server-side or prerendered output used when the observed compatibility need justifies it rather than as a universal requirement.

HTTP success must agree with the delivered content. Google documents that a `200` response containing an error, empty main content, or a failed render can be classified as a soft 404, while removed resources should return meaningful `404` or `410` responses and moved resources should use an appropriate redirect. Conditional retrieval with `If-Modified-Since` or `If-None-Match` can legitimately yield `304` without a body. Temporary `429` or `503` responses indicate overload and invite retry, but persistent unavailability can reduce crawling or remove URLs from Google's index. Record response time, body state, and recurrence before distinguishing a transient failure from persistent access loss. [S45](#s45)

Localized pages require coherent mappings. Google's documented `hreflang` contract requires fully qualified alternates, self-inclusion, and reciprocal links, with valid language-region codes and optional `x-default`; Google detects page language from content rather than relying on `lang` or `hreflang`. Same-language regional duplicates should combine compatible canonical and locale signals instead of collapsing every locale to a global URL.

Gated content needs an explicit publishing decision. Google's paywall markup applies to content a publisher wants crawled and indexed and distinguishes that arrangement from cloaking. Markup should match the visible access boundary, and a publisher that does not want protected text delivered to the browser should use an implementation that withholds it rather than exposing it client-side for extraction.

Structured-data verdicts must name their tool. Google's Rich Results Test reports which Google rich results the markup on a page can generate, while the Schema Markup Validator validates schema.org-based markup without Google feature-specific warnings—Google having removed Google-specific validation from the older testing tool when it migrated to that validator. Search Console's rich result reports describe a third thing again, drawn from what Google has already processed. A pass in any of them establishes only what that tool measures; none reports indexing, display, or citation. [S04](#s04) [S63](#s63)

Entity disambiguation has a documented markup path and an undocumented outcome. Google asks for organization structured data on the home page or a single page describing the organization rather than on every page, lists identifier properties such as `iso6523Code`, `naics`, `taxID`, `vatID`, `globalLocationNumber`, and `leiCode` as behind-the-scenes disambiguation signals, describes `sameAs` as pointing to other sites carrying additional information about the organization, and states that features consuming structured data are not guaranteed to appear. Google separately says knowledge panels are generated automatically once enough information exists on the open web, draw on many sources including licensed data and facts supplied by content owners, and can be claimed and corrected by a verified representative. Panels can appear, change, or stop appearing without any publisher action. Treat identifier and `sameAs` coverage as reconcilable evidence work, and treat a knowledge panel as an outcome to observe rather than a deliverable to promise. [S55](#s55) [S56](#s56)

Agent-facing tool surfaces are engineering, not visibility work. WebMCP lets a page register named tools with natural-language descriptions, JSON Schema inputs, and handlers so that browser and extension agents can actuate an interface directly, with the specification describing such pages as Model Context Protocol servers implementing tools in client-side script instead of on the backend. Its own status boilerplate says it "is not a W3C Standard nor is it on the W3C Standards Track," and the current text is a Draft Community Group Report dated 26 August 2026. Chrome's accompanying security guidance notes repeatable prompt-injection attacks against agentic systems, recommends an untrusted-content hint when a tool returns user-generated or external data, uses a read-only hint so agents can decide when to ask for confirmation, and warns to "only expose your tools to origins that you trust." Treat tool exposure as an application-security decision with the same untrusted-input discipline the rest of this review applies to retrieved content. [S68](#s68) [S69](#s69)

Agentic checkout is a further distinct distribution path. OpenAI's Agentic Commerce Protocol has merchants supply a regularly refreshed CSV or JSON feed of identifiers, pricing, availability, media, and fulfillment data for discovery, and describes recommended attributes as improving "ranking, relevance, and user trust" without promising display or purchasability. The merchant keeps the substantive obligations—it "performs validation, determines fulfillment options, calculates and charges sales tax," analyzes payment and risk on its own stack, and brings its own payment provider—and OpenAI states it "is not the merchant of record." A feed integration therefore neither replaces the public product page nor guarantees that a product is shown or buyable, and its freshness must be managed separately. [S70](#s70)

Agent-initiated payment is a separate accountability problem again. Google's Agent Payments Protocol, published at version 0.2 with the FIDO Alliance and the Agent2Agent community and described as continuing standardization inside FIDO working groups, addresses authorization, authenticity, and accountability when an agent rather than a person initiates a purchase. It represents user authorization as verifiable digital credentials—"tamper-evident, cryptographically signed digital objects"—in checkout and payment mandates that capture constraints before finalization and authorization afterwards, chained into an auditable record. This is vendor-led work moving toward formal standardization, and it governs who authorized and is answerable for a transaction rather than whether a product is retrievable or cited. Record the arrangement and verify it with the merchant; do not treat it as a visibility surface. [S73](#s73)

For local and commerce questions, reconcile visible facts and applicable structured data with owner-controlled profiles and feeds such as Google Business Profile, Merchant Center, and Bing Places. These are conditional distribution surfaces, not generic requirements for every site.

Rich-result features are withdrawn as well as added, so eligibility is a perishable fact. Google narrowed FAQ rich results so that "the feature is only shown for well-known, authoritative government and health websites," and deprecated How-to entirely, removing its structured data documentation because "this rich result is no longer shown in search results, on both desktop and mobile devices." Neither type appears in Google's current gallery of supported structured data features, which was last updated in June 2026 and lists roughly two dozen result types. An audit or patch must therefore check the current supported-feature list at execution time rather than trusting an older recommendation, and must not present any markup type as a durable answer-engine tactic. Unused markup left in place is a separate question from whether the feature still exists. [S66](#s66) [S67](#s67)

Review structured data has feature-specific rules beyond schema validity. Google requires marked-up review content and ratings to be visible, tied to a specific item, and not aggregated from other websites; it excludes fake or undisclosed incentivized reviews and makes self-controlled `LocalBusiness` or `Organization` reviews ineligible for its star review feature. These are Google rich-result eligibility rules, not proof that a review is lawful, independent, trustworthy, ranked, or cited elsewhere. [S50](#s50)

Commerce distribution is product-specific. Google describes product structured data and Merchant Center feeds as complementary inputs for its eligible shopping experiences. OpenAI says ChatGPT shopping can use merchant, partner, and public-web data, with Shopify catalog integration and direct feeds available in applicable programs. Price, availability, variants, shipping, and returns are volatile; agents should verify the final merchant destination and keep each applicable surface's data synchronized without assuming one feed propagates everywhere.

Crawler identity cannot be established from a user-agent header. Google explicitly warns that Googlebot user agents are spoofed and documents verification through current published IP ranges or forward-confirmed reverse DNS. The same evidence rule applies wherever an operator publishes a verification mechanism: a manual request with a crawler user agent tests response behavior, but it is not evidence that the real crawler made or can make that request from its production network.

Crawler identification is gaining a cryptographic option alongside address-based checks. A protocol draft by authors at Cloudflare and Google has automated clients sign requests using HTTP Message Signatures, carrying `Signature`, `Signature-Input`, and a `Signature-Agent` header that points at the operator's key directory; it mandates asymmetric algorithms such as Ed25519 or RSA-PSS rather than shared secrets, resolves keys through a well-known `http-message-signatures-directory` JWKS endpoint or an equivalent, and requires `created` and `expires` parameters with recommended expiry windows no longer than a day. The document is revision 02 dated 18 August 2026, is an individual submission intended for the Standards Track, is not an RFC, and replaces an earlier architecture draft that has expired. Cloudflare has deployed the mechanism inside its Verified Bots program using Ed25519 and a signed JWKS directory, states that it "relies on IETF drafts," and falls back to other methods "such as reverse DNS validation" when signature verification is disabled. Signature verification therefore identifies a client per request rather than a network per address, and it supplements rather than replaces published IP ranges and forward-confirmed reverse DNS. An unsigned request remains unverified, not proven illegitimate. [S71](#s71) [S72](#s72)

Owner diagnostics also have distinct time semantics. Google Search Console's indexed URL Inspection result describes the last indexed version and can report Google's selected canonical; its live test evaluates a current fetch but omits canonical selection, sitemap/referrer discovery, quality and security policies, manual actions, and other index-time states. Audits must preserve both timestamps and must not promote a successful live test into an indexing claim.

Evidence can live in media, but media-only claims are fragile. Google uses surrounding page text, captions, filenames, alt text, structured data, landing pages, thumbnails, and accessible media URLs to understand images and videos. These fields should describe the same visible subject for users and crawlers; alt text should remain useful accessibility text rather than a hidden keyword or claim channel.

Internal discovery requires links that crawlers and people can follow. Google documents `<a>` elements with resolvable `href` values as the reliable link contract and recommends at least one internal link to every important page. Script-only click handlers, router-specific attributes without `href`, and fragments that replace primary content are not equivalent. Sitemaps remain useful discovery hints but do not replace coherent navigation.

Crawl budget is a conditional diagnosis, not a default AEO project. Google's current guide is aimed primarily at million-page sites with regular changes, sites with tens of thousands of rapidly changing pages, or sites with substantial “Discovered - currently not indexed” inventory. Smaller sites whose new pages are crawled promptly generally need accurate sitemaps and indexing diagnostics, not attempts to increase crawl rate; crawling itself is not a ranking outcome.

Response variation must be interpreted by intent and context. Locale, geography, account state, subscription state, device, and personalization can legitimately change a page. Serving materially different evidence specifically to a crawler for manipulation is cloaking. Audits should compare like-for-like contexts and improvements should preserve genuine user-visible behavior rather than create crawler-only variants.

`llms.txt` deserves an accurate description rather than a reflex. It is a community convention, first published in September 2024 and revised in August 2026, specifying a Markdown file at `/llms.txt` or a subpath whose only required element is an H1 naming the project, conventionally followed by a blockquote summary, free-form sections, and H2-delimited link lists, with an `Optional` section holding links an agent may skip under a shorter context. Its stated audience is language models and agents reading a site at inference time. Google Search still says the file neither helps nor hurts Search visibility, and no source connects it to answer-engine citation. What has changed is that a named tool now consumes it: Chrome's Lighthouse checks `llms.txt` discoverability inside an Agentic Browsing category. That satisfies the "demonstrated consuming system" condition for browser-agent work without turning the file into a search or citation tactic. [S01](#s01) [S61](#s61) [S62](#s62)

That Lighthouse category should be read carefully. It runs deterministic audits covering accessibility-tree names, labels, tree integrity and visibility, layout stability, `llms.txt` discoverability, and declarative or imperative WebMCP tool registration. Google states that it "does not have a weighted average score from 0 to 100" because "the standards for the agentic web are still emerging," and describes the category and WebMCP support as "experimental and based on proposed standards," with the aim of gathering data and offering actionable signals rather than a definitive ranking. Passing these audits is a build-quality signal for machine interaction. It is not evidence of indexing, ranking, answer selection, or citation, and the underlying proposals may change. [S62](#s62)

Do not recommend `llms.txt`, FAQ blocks, tiny "AI chunks," schema, or one page per query variation by default. A site may maintain `llms.txt` for a tool that explicitly consumes it, but Google states that it neither helps nor hurts Google Search visibility. Avoid scaled query pages, hidden text, keyword stuffing, fake freshness, purchased or fabricated mentions, and crawler-specific deceptive content.

Google's current spam policies define scaled content abuse by purpose and lack of user value rather than by whether humans or generative tools created it. They also prohibit publishing third-party content mainly to exploit the host site's established ranking signals. AEO work should not turn prompt corpora, feeds, translations, or partner content into mass-produced landing pages without original value and accountable editorial ownership.

### 4. Measurement contract

Use an evidence ladder and never blend its levels into one score:

1. **Deterministic verification:** HTTP and rendered properties, crawler directives, visible content, structured-data parity, source links, build results, and feed/profile consistency.
2. **Owner telemetry:** Google Search Console's generative AI report, Bing Webmaster Tools AI Performance, verified referral analytics, and server logs. Record each product's aggregation, sampling, rollout, and privacy limitations.
3. **Controlled simulation:** an API or agent receives a fixed prompt and an eligible evidence snapshot. This tests comprehension under stated conditions, not performance on a consumer product.
4. **Public-surface observation:** a named consumer surface returns an answer under recorded account, locale, device, access, and time conditions.
5. **Business outcome:** verified downstream visits, conversions, revenue, qualified leads, or another predeclared outcome.

Owner search telemetry is deliberately incomplete, and its shape matters when an agent exports it. Google documents that on the query tab "anonymized (rare) results are omitted from the table, but are included in the chart totals unless a query filter is applied," that "the table can display a maximum of 1,000 rows, so some rare or long-tail rows might be omitted from the table, but included in the chart total," and that when filtering by page or query the "match" and "does not match" totals "may not add up to the unfiltered total, due to the omission of anonymized queries and data truncation." Summed export rows are therefore not a total, a filtered comparison is not a partition, and an absent query row is not evidence of zero impressions. Record which filters were applied and keep chart totals and table rows as separate quantities. [S65](#s65)

For prompt observations, store the immutable prompt and hash, provider, product surface, displayed model when available, search or grounding mode, account state, locale, geography, device, timestamp, sample number, raw answer, citations, failures, parser version, and extraction result. Separate:

- brand mention;
- link to the canonical brand domain;
- citations that actually support a statement about the brand;
- citations to third-party sources near a brand mention;
- source ownership and domain;
- accepted negative answers, abstentions, and technical failures.

Qualitative labels such as full or partial claim support require more than an undocumented reviewer impression. NIST's ARIA pilot used a defined annotation schema, independent annotators, calibration, recorded disagreement, and adjudication; disagreement was treated as evidence for edge cases and instruction refinement. For material citation-support review, preserve the rubric version, reviewer condition, disagreement, and adjudication outcome. This improves repeatability but does not make a subjective label objectively true. [S47](#s47)

For interactive pages, isolated ARIA presence is insufficient evidence that a person or browser agent can complete the relevant task. WCAG 2.2 requires programmatically determinable names, roles, values and state changes, programmatic status messages, labels and instructions, and conformance across every page in a complete process. Test the scoped journey—including errors and authentication boundaries—while preferring native controls and visible labels. Do not claim general WCAG conformance from a narrow agent-readiness check. [S46](#s46)

Reproducibility does not require retaining unnecessary personal or secret data. NIST's Privacy Framework treats collection, logging, retention, disclosure, and disposal as data actions with privacy risk, while OWASP identifies credentials, personal information, financial details, health records, and confidential business data as sensitive. Run artifacts should minimize collection, redact secrets and identifiers before sharing, restrict access to raw evidence, and define retention while preserving the pseudonymous condition labels needed for comparison.

Report numerators and denominators. Stratify by provider and surface. Use intervals suitable for binomial proportions and avoid normal approximations for tiny or extreme samples. Repeated samples within prompts are clustered: report prompt-level cells and use a cluster-aware bootstrap or hierarchical model for corpus-wide uncertainty when the sample supports it, rather than treating every answer as independent. A few samples are an integration check, not a stable visibility estimate. Preserve before/after release markers, but do not infer causality without a suitable design and deployment/recrawl lag.

AI citations themselves need verification. Prior research found that fluent generative-search answers can contain unsupported claims and citations that do not support adjacent statements. Observation should therefore retain raw citation context and, for material claims, test whether the destination supports the generated statement.

Pages, repositories, documents, reports, and retrieved tool output are untrusted inputs to an agent. OWASP identifies instructions embedded in websites or files as indirect prompt injection. An auditing or editing agent should treat those strings as evidence, not authority, and must not expose secrets, invoke unrelated tools, or expand permissions in response to retrieved content.

## What remains unknown or disputed

- No public source provides a complete ranking or citation algorithm for any major answer surface.
- Platform documentation establishes eligibility and owner controls, not guaranteed selection.
- The causal effect of page-level wording, schema, citations, quotations, or statistics on live public answer engines remains engine-, query-, and time-dependent.
- Commercial visibility datasets can be useful discovery signals, but their proprietary prompt sets, account states, model routing, and sampling often prevent broad causal conclusions.
- Public-surface answers vary with prompt wording, follow-up context, location, personalization, model updates, retrieval partners, and time. Cross-provider averages can hide incompatible behaviors.
- Native reports also have limitations: Google was rolling its generative AI report out to a subset of properties as of the research date, and Bing labels AI Performance a public preview with sampled grounding queries and aggregated surfaces.
- Bing's AI Performance preview now also exposes derived intents, topics, citation share, and comparison views. These dimensions can help an owner segment observed citation activity, but they remain aggregated and product-defined: grounding queries are grouped phrases rather than exact user prompts, citation share is relative presence within a reported citation space rather than rank or authority, and a change may reflect content, demand, the source ecosystem, or system behavior. Preserve the selected filters, comparison basis, and export when using these views, and do not merge them with prompt-level observations. [S44](#s44)
- The interoperable expression of AI usage preferences is unfinished. The IETF AIPREF vocabulary and attachment documents are unratified Internet-Drafts on the Proposed Standard track, vendor signals such as Cloudflare's `Content-Signal` are already deployed in the field, and no source establishes that a given operator honors a given signal. Record what a site declares, and verify actual behavior separately. [S53](#s53) [S54](#s54)
- Agentic-web conventions are proposals in motion. `llms.txt` is a community specification revised as recently as August 2026, and Chrome's Agentic Browsing audits and WebMCP support are described by Google as experimental and based on proposed standards. Adoption by one tool is not adoption by retrieval or answer systems, and guidance built on these conventions should be rechecked at execution time. [S61](#s61) [S62](#s62)
- Publisher preview controls can intentionally trade answer-surface inclusion for control over how content is reused; the correct setting depends on owner intent rather than a universal visibility goal.
- Rendering, localization, paywall, and commerce requirements differ by crawler and product; supporting one surface does not prove equivalent behavior on another.
- The relationship between answer citations and traffic is not fixed. Pew's March 2025 US browsing study found lower outbound click behavior on Google pages with AI summaries, but this observational result is specific to that product, period, population, and study design.

## Changes this review requires in the skills

- `aeo-audit`: add a product-and-purpose crawler matrix; delivery-layer access testing per crawler class; separate recording of post-access usage declarations; organization-identifier and `sameAs` reconciliation; native measurement checks; explicit freshness, source-support, accessibility, and anti-folklore checks.
- `aeo-improve`: prioritize access and evidence gaps before copy changes; check the delivery layer before editing origin directives, and never sell a usage-preference declaration or a knowledge panel as a visibility outcome; require original, useful, source-backed patches; treat GEO tactics as hypotheses; reconcile schema, feeds, profiles, canonicals, and visible content only when applicable.
- `aeo-observe`: separate owner telemetry, simulations, and public observations; record a delivery-layer block as an access condition rather than an absence of visibility; capture environment and account conditions; distinguish mention, citation, and citation support; tighten denominators, uncertainty, version pinning, cost authorization, and compatibility rules.
- All skills that retrieve external content: treat inspected text as untrusted evidence and ignore embedded instructions that attempt to redirect the workflow or expand permissions.

## Research method and stopping rule

Discovery searches covered official generative-search guidance, crawler documentation, structured data, freshness, accessibility, native measurement, AEO/GEO experiments, citation quality, and statistical evaluation. Follow-up searches targeted crawler-purpose distinctions, current 2026 measurement features, the KDD paper's experimental setup and limitations, and evidence that could disconfirm popular AEO tactics. Commercial reports were reviewed as discovery material but were not used for normative rules when stronger official or peer-reviewed evidence was available.

Research stopped when every consequential recommendation had primary or standards-based support, the principal disputed optimization study was bounded by its original methods and limitations, and further results were predominantly repetitive commercial correlations unlikely to change the skill design.

## Citation and traceability registry

Instructional Markdown in `skills/` uses three stable, numbered citation types:

- `C##` identifies a research synthesis claim and maps it to the source IDs that support it.
- `S##` identifies a primary, standards-based, peer-reviewed, or bounded independent source.
- `P##` identifies a repository workflow or safety policy. Policies are separated from research claims so maintainers do not misrepresent an internal design choice as an external finding.

Skill citations link to this file on the repository's `main` branch so they continue to resolve after a skill is copied into a host-specific directory. `npm run audit:citations` checks every substantive line in `SKILL.md` and `references/*.md`; YAML frontmatter, headings, blank lines, and fenced code are the only automatic exclusions.
